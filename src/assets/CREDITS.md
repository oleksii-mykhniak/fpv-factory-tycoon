# Asset Credits

Every sprite in `public/sprites/` is generated by
`scripts/gen-placeholder-sprites.js` — drones, boxes, the workbench, the walk
cycles, the cat, and (since V4) the four objects the player walks up to: the
desk, the upgrade rack, the job board and the bin. Those four had no PNG at all
until then; the loader was drawing its missing-sprite fallback rectangle.

The plan (`docs/plans/stage6_visual.md`, V4) is that FURNITURE and DECOR come
from CC0 packs while CHARACTERS stay on the generator — it does role palettes
and animation, which no ready-made pack will match. Nothing has been downloaded
yet: picking a pack means picking it whole, since two pieces of furniture from
different sets look worse side by side than two placeholders.

## CC0 / Third-party sprites

**None in use.** Three Kenney packs were downloaded and evaluated (RPG Urban,
Roguelike Indoors, Roguelike Modern City — all CC0 1.0) and then dropped: their
2D top-down art is 16px built around a 16px character, while ours is 64px, so
the same physical object arrived with pixels four times the size. Everything in
`public/sprites/` is generated by `scripts/gen-placeholder-sprites.js`.

## The rule that keeps it one style

Three things, and none of them is discipline:

1. **One palette.** `P` in the generator. Every object picks from it and from
   nowhere else. Floors have their own quieter shades so they sit under the
   furniture instead of competing with it.
2. **One light.** Only `box()` shades, and it always lights the top edge and
   shadows the bottom. Walls are drawn the same way in the scene, which is what
   stopped them looking like a different game from the floor.
3. **One pixel density.** Sprites declare their size in WORLD UNITS and the
   generator divides by `UNITS_PER_PX = 74/64`. A 148-unit bed is therefore a
   128px sprite, the same grain as the 74-unit character's 64px one. This is the
   rule the Kenney experiment broke, and the only one that cannot be fixed later
   by recolouring.
